# PNW wildlife and ambient life

Expanded PNW worlds now start with 128 Douglas's squirrels, correcting the low encounter density over the fourfold larger area. Legacy PNW runs receive 32 when opened, at the same population density, without changing their original map size or terrain. Three are seeded 8–20 metres from the starting player beside standing trees where suitable habitat exists; the remaining population is balanced across forest cells on dry, unobstructed ground near tree shelter. Existing hares, deer, fish and predator targets stay the same. Squirrels join the existing prey replenishment, hunting, skinning, butchering, skill, save and multiplayer systems. Both PNW generations receive the cosmetic atmosphere.

Previously created PNW saves receive the missing squirrel population once on Continue, Retry or Restart. The wildlife activation is independent of `pnwGen`, so Continue and New Run on a legacy world also work. Existing animals, terrain, tree/resource state, inventory and gameplay RNG are preserved when loading. A separate `pnwWildlife: 1` save marker prevents subsequent loads from replenishing hunted squirrels. Multiplayer guests receive the host's animals and do not initialize a second population.

Squirrels have 0.6 health versus the hare's 1.0, a smaller collision body, fast bounding runs and quicker turns. They pause and forage near home, watch approaching players, and run to a nearby standing tree when threatened. They climb the trunk, hide in its canopy while danger remains, then descend headfirst. Tree refuges are also used at night; they do not hibernate. Unreachable or felled refuges cannot trap the animal. Ground and climbing squirrels can be hunted; hidden squirrels cannot be targeted. A climbing kill leaves its carcass on accessible ground beside the tree. Saved tree indices and elevation preserve the escape state; multiplayer protocol 12 carries elevation and climbing/hiding modes to guests.

Butterflies and dragonflies are cosmetic render objects, with no combat, loot or interaction entries. Their RNG is separate from gameplay. Butterflies flutter, land at actual flower tips to feed, follow the flower's wind movement, and rest on dry rocks and logs. Dragonflies use water-edge habitats, alternate patrols with faster darts and short stationary hovers, and perch with their wings extended. Flight timings and population caps are gameplay approximations.

Butterfly wings span about 8.5 cm. Dragonflies have a roughly 7 cm body and 9 cm wingspan. Arrival effects never enlarge them beyond that scale. At most 10 butterflies and 6 dragonflies are maintained near the camera. New flight homes are within 10 metres, favoring the player's view, rather than randomly distributed up to 34 metres away. Coordinate-seeded dry flight stations provide habitat between decorative objects; butterflies can fly without requiring a nearby flower, but can only feed on actual blooms. Dragonfly habitat includes water-adjacent flight stations. Empty-air stations are never used as landing surfaces. Effects outside the encounter area or left behind the camera are recycled with opacity fading.

Insects appear only in sunlight: 07:00–18:00 on sunny nonwinter days, using the game's clock offset. They are absent at night, in rain, fog, cloud cover or snow. PNW night fireflies are replaced by the requested wisps so the forest has no nocturnal insect effect.

Up to two soft blue-green wisps float slowly near dry forest habitat throughout the day and night, including winter. Homes are selected within 5–12 metres with room for their small orbit, favoring the player's view. They use small additive sprites with distance fading and no point lights or interaction. Desert and Island atmosphere and populations remain unchanged.

## Behavior research

- [National Park Service: Douglas's squirrel](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/douglas-s-squirrel.htm): native conifer habitat, tawny belly, size up to 37 cm including the tail, daytime foraging, tree shelter, year-round activity and climbing adaptations.
- [Butterflies and Moths of North America: Western Tiger Swallowtail](https://www.butterfliesandmoths.org/species/Papilio-rutulus): western riparian woodland habitat, flower nectar and 7–10 cm wingspan.
- [USDA: Native Pollinators](https://efotg.sc.egov.usda.gov/references/public/SC/Native_Pollinators.pdf): nectar plants and warming on sunlit surfaces before flight.
- [Minnesota Dragonfly Society: behavior](https://www.mndragonfly.org/html/behavior.html): aerial hunting, perching and retreat to protected perches in cool conditions.
- [Minnesota Dragonfly Society: Blue-eyed Darner](https://mndragonfly.info/html/darner/blue-eyed.html): adult dimensions and strong flight. The game's dragonflies combine typical patrol, hover and perch behavior rather than simulate one species' entire lifecycle.

## Nonvisual validation

Behavior tests cover escape at multiple update rates, canopy safety, return to the ground, felled refuges, damage and arrows, carcasses, population seeding/replenishment, saves and wire poses. Ambient checks cover feeding/resting, flower sway, patrol/hover/dart/perch phases, dimensions, deterministic behavior, sunlight gating, effect caps and daytime/nighttime wisps. Encounter regression checks project actual positions into the starting camera, measure apparent size mathematically, test moving/turning, and verify one-time save migration and unobstructed squirrel placement. Production browser smoke checks observe actual world-camera draw submissions, geometric obstruction rays and JavaScript/WebGL errors. No screenshots, pixel reads or visual inspection are used.
